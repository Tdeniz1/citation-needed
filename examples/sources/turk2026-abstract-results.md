Across 6 configurations and 4 conditions we scored 43,508 emitted citations from 9,284 responses over 400 claims. In the parametric baseline, fabricated references accounted for 7.13% of citations (95% CI 6.68 to 7.62), retracted references for 0.21% (0.14 to 0.31), and materially distorted references for 85.40% (84.74 to 86.03). At least one fabricated or retracted citation appeared in 20.88% (19.30 to 22.55) of baseline responses. Distortion was dominated by one mechanism, in that 76.5% of distorted citations carried an identifier that resolved to a record whose title did not match the reference the model wrote, a failure an existence-only audit scores as a success. Across the four open-weight models the response-level bad-citation rate fell steeply with parameter count up to 70 billion and then flattened, in that the 8-billion-parameter model trailed the best of them by 20.4 points pooled across conditions (29.06% versus 8.62%) while the 70-billion and 120-billion models were indistinguishable from each other (8.62% versus 9.31%). Tool use reduced the fabrication odds by 79.1% relative to baseline (adjusted OR 0.17, 95% CI 0.15 to 0.20, Holm-adjusted P < .001) and retrieval grounding by 49.9% (OR 0.50, 0.45 to 0.56, Holm-adjusted P < .001), while prompted self-verification more than doubled them (OR 2.12, 2.02 to 2.24, Holm-adjusted P < .001) and raised fabrication from 7.13% to 16.24%. No intervention significantly changed the retracted-citation rate. The intervention ranking did not hold across models, in that tool use raised the response-level bad rate for gpt-oss-120b from 9.50% to 15.00%, the worst tool-use cell in the matrix. Error rates on false claims were not higher than on well-supported claims, at 14.54% versus 14.39% of responses carrying a fabricated or retracted citation.
